This is actually my third website. 2026 has been quite a year for me: I've built lots of projects, tried new tech stacks, and kept exploring the open-source community (I even got [a warm reply from a maintainer at Microsoft](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode-documentdb/pull/670#issuecomment-4553370550)). I've been wandering through the wider world of development, just following my curiosity, with no particular payoff in mind. One day, it occurred to me: "I should write some technical posts of my own!" So I built this site using Astro, with help from GPT 6.1 Sol.

## "Programming Is Dead"

Honestly, AI has gone well beyond what we imagined. Three years ago, building a website while following online tutorials still felt like a real accomplishment. Now Claude and ChatGPT have taken over a sizable chunk of what frontend engineers used to do, and I can put together a fun site like this just by describing what I want in plain language. Jensen Huang showed off Hugging Face's Microduck at a launch event. Yesterday, Google released its latest world model, Genie 4. We have no idea what the future will look like, or which field will have its "GPT moment" next.

> What can an ordinary person—a student in a tech-related field—still do to keep up with these dizzying changes?

**I don't know.** But I've spent a lot of time exploring new technologies and directions, the way an ordinary, earnest undergrad might have back in the 2010s. Enthusiasm doesn't always get you as far as you'd hoped, though. It's hard to compare myself with classmates who are focused on results, competing for publications and internships. Sometimes I feel pushed into an endless chase, constantly having to make myself look better on paper. With fewer jobs and more people competing for them, I find myself hesitating, wondering what kind of life I actually want. But this isn't really the place to get into that.

## From Low to Ultra

Autumn is settling in, and winter won't be far behind. I think back six years, to the start of the pandemic, to the days I spent learning linear algebra so I could get a handle on new algorithms. Algorithm research isn't really what I want to do, and I don't think I have any interest in that kind of mathematical reasoning. But life moved at a slower pace then. There was still plenty of time to explore, study, and learn, without the pressure to rush or make everything pay off, whether it came from outside or from myself.

I come back to the present. Codex has written another 1,300 lines of code, and I've long since lost interest in reading through it.

**The last program I wrote entirely by hand was for a course project in 2024.** It's a sobering thought, isn't it?
